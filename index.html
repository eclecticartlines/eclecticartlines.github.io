How these are built

Four rules, and no exceptions to them

The same four decisions sit under every app in the series. They are what the apps are for

Nothing leaves the phone. Not as a promise in a policy page: there is no internet permission in the manifest at all. Recognition and translation models ship inside the app. No server exists to be breached, and no account exists to be lost.

Figures are computed, never guessed. Where a number is unknown, the app says so out loud instead of filling the gap with a zero. A zero would flatter the owner and collapse the moment anyone checked.

Every reference number carries its source. The tables behind the arithmetic are not written from memory; each value is checked by a test against the range its source gives.

Decisions are written down as they are made — what was built, what was refused, and the reason for both. Refusals included: a good idea that breaks one of the rules above is still refused.

What the entire team would have done

Four departments, one desk

1

Idea

I build what I needed myself and couldn’t find: a box for the gold, a scanned book that reads itself aloud, a record of what is in the house.

2

Design

Screens, icon sets, store graphics. Seventeen years of layout and pre-press before any of this — calm colours, large type, nothing that shouts.

3

Build

Unity for the games, Flutter for the apps. Everything on the device: no server to go down, no account to lose, no cloud to trust.

4

Ship

Listing, screenshots and description written out by hand in 24 languages. Then the support letters — answered by the person who wrote the code.

About

Building the kind of apps people assume require an entire team

Automation, privacy and real-world data — the three things that usually take a department each. Here they are one person’s work: the idea, the design, the code, the testing, the release, and the store listing written out in 24 languages.

I come from graphic design and pre-press production, so I care a lot about how things look and feel: calm colours, clean screens, nothing that shouts at you. And because nothing leaves your phone, privacy isn’t a policy page — it’s how the apps are built.
